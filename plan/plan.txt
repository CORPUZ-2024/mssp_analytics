Performance year 2024 uses a 67% V24 / 33% V28 blend, mirroring the Medicare Advantage transition. V28 removed or downweighted vascular disease and certain metabolic conditions. Risk score compression from V28 is methodological, not clinical — counties with high concentrations of those conditions will show score drops unrelated to population health. MSSP does NOT retroactively recalculate benchmark year risk scores under model changes, introducing version skew across data cuts.Enrollment type segmentationThe PUF separates four enrollment types: ESRD (End-Stage Renal Disease), Disabled, Aged Dual-Eligible, and Aged Non-Dual. Each carries different risk profiles, expenditure levels, and analytical stability. Aged Non-Dual and Aged Dual have the largest N and most stable estimates. The revised focus moves to expenditure efficiency characterization, which is more stable under methodology changes.Core metric — Expenditure Efficiency RatioFormulaExpenditure Efficiency Ratio = PER_CAPITA_EXP / AVG_RISK_SCORE   (per county, per year, per enrollment type)Declining ratio over time signals genuine expenditure improvement harder to attribute to methodology change, since both numerator and denominator are affected by the same rule changes in the same direction. Calculate separately for each of the four enrollment types. Use person-years columns as weights for volatility-adjusted analysis.3.2  What a seasoned researcher would do differentlyConstruct county relative cost index: risk-standardized county expenditure / risk-standardized national expenditure by enrollment typeUse person-years columns as weights — a county with 5 ESRD person-years has volatile estimates; one with 5,000 has stable onesFocus quantitative claims on Aged Non-Dual and Aged Dual (largest N); treat ESRD and Disabled as supplementary or regional aggregatesLink to PY Financial &amp; Quality Results PUF (data.cms.gov) — enables: in counties where risk-adjusted expenditure exceeded national average, did ACOs still generate savings?Segment by agreement period start date — ACOs subject to 2024 benchmarking had net savings per capita of $143 vs. $241 for those not subject to it; naive YoY comparison conflates methodology with performanceApply truncation sensitivity analysis — CMS truncates beneficiary expenditures at 99th percentile; threshold varies by year and enrollment typeDifference-in-differences using AHEAD/TEAM pilot rollout — plausible comparison groups for quasi-experimental design; potentially publishable04  Four analytical modules — plan, metrics &amp; benchmarksBuild sequenceTrack 2 first (HCC risk flags) → Track 3 (shared savings model) → Tracks 1 + 4 in parallel.
